The Vault — put a real number into the codec and be tested on it days later, as the encoding intends.

Stored numbers

    Store a number

    Sealed entries are the safe default for anything private. Everything in this app lives in unencrypted browser storage and travels in your exported save file — so a memory trainer is exactly the wrong place to leave a PIN or a card number lying about in plain text. A sealed entry stores only the length and the per-locus seals, never the digits: the app then cannot show you the answer, because it does not have it, and your recall is checked by recomputing the seals. That is a checksum and not a hash — it catches a wrong locus roughly eight times in nine (fourteen in fifteen for hex), which is useful evidence, not proof. Store in full only what you would not mind someone reading.

    Retention

    A new entry is due immediately — the first recall belongs in the session that created it, while the scenes are still warm, because that is what turns an encoding into a memory. After that the gap expands: 1 day, 3 days, then multiplying by an ease that drifts up with each success, out to a year. A miss collapses the gap back to one day and drops the ease harder than a success raises it, so a number you keep half-knowing keeps coming back.

    When a recall fails, the seals say which locus broke, not what it should have been. That is deliberate and it is the whole use of the checksum: you go back to the scene for that locus and repair it yourself. Rebuilding a corrupt image is what fixes it — being told the digits is not.
