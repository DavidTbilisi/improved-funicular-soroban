Flash anzan — the board with nothing to look at

·

Pick a rung. Each number is lit for most of its interval and then blanked — the gap is what makes two equal numbers in a row countable, so do not read it as flicker. You cannot answer until the last one has gone. A round is right or wrong, nothing in between; carry the rung's whole streak unbroken and the pace you did it at is banked as your record for that shape. Speed changes apply to the next round, never to a flash you are already reading.
The three axes are independent: how many numbers, how wide each one is, and how fast they come. The ladder walks the first two; the speed control is yours. Missing a round shows the terms back — read the sequence and find where the board slipped, because that is the only feedback anzan can give you.

Why this is the last page

Flash anzan is not a separate trick — it is the guided practice ladder with the board taken away and a clock started. The Mental stage of the mnemonic-mental track is the prerequisite: there, the rods are imagined but you set the pace; here the pace is imposed and there is nothing to peek at.

So the arithmetic is the same arithmetic. A miss on 5 × 1 digit is a ten-complement you have not automated; a miss on 5 × 2 digits is usually a carry that arrived while you were still setting the ones rod. When a rung stalls, the fix is normally on the practice page, not here — the Today page names the complement pair your fumbles point at.
